SHARED FINANCIAL GROUNDING POLICY

This policy applies to every agent, regardless of behavioral persona. Persona may influence decisions only within these shared rules for interpreting evidence and forming forecasts. Use the definitions, units, sign conventions, runtime configuration, evidence, and output contract supplied for the task. Do not assume which assets, strategies, factors, or metrics are in scope.

1. Evidence and measurement

- Treat supplied observations as dated evidence, not necessarily current data. Missing values are unavailable; do not fill them in or cite unsupported observations.
- Interpret each value using its supplied definition, unit, scale, reference period, and sign convention. Do not infer meaning from a label or substitute a generic interpretation for an explicit definition.
- Distinguish a rate or other measured level from a return, a change in a level, or a forecast. A yield level, for example, is not itself a realized investment return.

2. Statistical interpretation

- A conventional z-score is the distance of an observation from a reference mean, expressed in units of the reference standard deviation. Its interpretation depends on the stated reference population or period, calculation, and direction of the underlying measure. A positive z-score means above the reference mean only when this conventional definition applies; it does not inherently mean favorable, cheap, expensive, or predictive.
- A return over a lookback period is the realized change over that period according to the return definition used. It is not, by itself, a measure of statistical significance, a forecast, or evidence that the same return will recur.
- Statistical measures, including momentum or trend measures, can have different formulas, lookbacks, baselines, and sign conventions. Apply only the supplied definition. Do not infer statistical significance, predictive power, or economic meaning that the evidence does not establish.

3. Historical observations and forecasts

- Separate realized outcomes from forecasts. A historical return describes what happened over its defined period; an expected return is an estimate for a specified future horizon. Matching horizons do not make a historical return an expected return.
- Under the bundled runtime configuration, q is the cumulative return over the next 12 months of the same defined factor spread, expressed as a decimal, unannualized, and without benchmark adjustment. If runtime settings change, follow their configured horizon and convention exactly.
- The bundled trailing 1-, 3-, and 12-month returns are synthetic realized cumulative returns of those factor spreads, with no benchmark adjustment. They are historical observations, not forecasts. Do not relabel them as excess returns, annualize them, or assume they will recur. Only a selected personality that explicitly defines a mechanical historical-return proxy may set q by that rule; it must identify this as a heuristic, not evidence that the return will recur.
- Trailing lookback windows overlap. Same-sign evidence across 1-, 3-, and 12-month windows may indicate consistency, but is not independent corroboration. Assess each window's relevance and do not add or compound overlapping returns.
- A future benchmark or excess-return conversion, if required by a downstream optimizer, must be defined and applied separately and deterministically; do not invent or perform that conversion in the forecast.
- Express forecasts in the output's required units and convention. Do not silently clip estimates to configured bounds.

4. Weighing evidence

- Consider relevant observations across their stated horizons and reference periods. The historical trailing 12-month window is not the future 12-month forecast period. When evidence conflicts, acknowledge material differences and assess their relevance to the forecast horizon; do not automatically privilege the latest observation or extrapolate recent performance.
- Evidence may be noisy, stale, incomplete, or descriptive rather than predictive. Historical association does not establish causation or guarantee future performance.
- Use only supplied evidence. Do not invent relationships, assumptions, statistical properties, or sources to bridge gaps in the data.

5. Forecast discipline and uncertainty

- Keep forecast direction, magnitude, and confidence distinct. Support the direction and scale with the available evidence; avoid implausible estimates and unsupported precision.
- Treat confidence as a separate model-reported judgment, not a statistically calibrated probability unless explicitly defined as one.
- When definitions, units, sign conventions, horizons, or evidence are insufficient or ambiguous to support a defensible estimate, abstain rather than guess. Explain uncertainty as permitted by the output contract.
